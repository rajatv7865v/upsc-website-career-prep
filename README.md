### Website Tab & Navigation Structure

- Agreed tab order: Home → About Us → Current Affairs → Syllabus/Strategy → Geography → Economy → (other sections)
- Geography and Economy to be **separate tabs**, each with Prelims and Mains sub-sections
- "About Us" should appear right after the Home tab

### Current Affairs Organization

- **Single Current Affairs tab** to replace the previously duplicated tabs
- Articles will be organized with **two layers of tagging**:
    - By exam stage: Prelims or Mains
    - By subject: Geography, Economy, Polity, IR, Environment, etc.
- Tagged articles automatically appear in the relevant subject tabs as well as the main current affairs tab
- Example: A Prelims article on the President would appear under Current Affairs (Prelims) **and** under the Polity subject tab
- Students with a weak subject can go to the subject tab to read only those current affairs; daily revisers can use the general tab

### Syllabus & Strategy Section

- "Pattern" tab to be renamed and split into **Syllabus** and **Strategy**
- Syllabus to include: GS Paper 1, GS Paper 2, GS Paper 3, GS Paper 4, and Prelims (GS + CSAT paragraphs)
- Content to be copied directly from the UPSC official website — plain, simple language with no jargon or fancy headings
- Strategy will be subject/paper-specific; syllabus and strategy kept as two separate entries

### Article Layout & Homepage Design

- Homepage should feature **2–3 highlighted/featured articles** prominently, with smaller articles in the corners — similar to newspaper websites
- A "latest article" button or "trending topics" section to be added to the homepage
- Individual article pages to display **Next** and **Previous** article navigation with visible headings
- Article suggestion section label to be simplified (current label "more guides for serious aspirants" to be replaced)
- Card-style layout for current affairs with highlighted headings that expand to full article content

### Content Philosophy & Branding

- The website should **not be overtly UPSC-branded**, especially in the early phase
- Content should feel accessible to general readers (e.g., IT professionals) as knowledge-driven articles, not exam-prep material
- Once content volume improves, the focus can shift more explicitly to UPSC
- Instructor plans to add content gradually and update/edit it over time; the current syllabus content is a starting point, not final

### Previous Year Questions (PYQ)

- PYQ section to be added for both Prelims and Mains
- Two formats to be uploaded: **scanned PDF** (from UPSC website) and **text-based PDF** (copy-paste formatted)
- Instructor has year-wise PYQs ready to upload

### Contact & Social Links

- Social links to include: Instagram, YouTube, Telegram, WhatsApp
- LinkedIn to be excluded (personal)
- Physical address field to be hidden (no physical office)

### Deployment & Next Steps

- Developer to implement all discussed changes and reconnect with the instructor in **2–3 days**
- Instructor plans to use the website **personally for 1–2 months** to identify gaps before sharing with students
